This month

What your toddler
needs to learn

🍎

Real food, real vocabulary

Food is encountered multiple times every day. This month names what's already on the table — apple, banana, mango, lemon. High repetition, high relevance.

🔬

Inquiry thinking

At 16 months they can hold a question and wait for an answer. Does it sink or float? What colour inside? The beginning of scientific thinking.

🔪

Real tools, real tasks

A toddler-safe knife on soft fruit builds hand strength, bilateral coordination, and the sense that doing real things matters.

🎨

Colour through fruit

Red apple, yellow banana, orange mango, green avocado. Fruit is the richest natural colour vocabulary available. October colour month will build directly on what's established here.

Inquiry kitchen

The investigation

Each inquiry follows the same arc: observe the outside, predict, transform, discover the inside, compare, name the sound. Ask questions and wait. There are no wrong answers — only observations.

🍎

Apple

aeh · aeh · Apple
1What colour is it? Red.
2Feel it — smooth or bumpy? Smooth.
3Drop in a tub of water — sink or float? Floats.
4Cut with the toddler knife — what colour inside? Creamy white. Different from outside.
5What colour are the seeds? Brown.
6Does the seed sink or float? Sinks. Different from the apple.
7Smell it. Taste a piece. Sweet.
8This is aeh · aeh · Apple.
🍌

Banana

buh · buh · Banana
1What colour? Yellow.
2What shape? Curved — bendy.
3Peel it together — what's inside? White.
4Cut a piece with the toddler knife. Does it come apart easily? Yes — soft.
5Put a piece in water — sink or float? Sinks. Different from apple.
6Mash a piece with fingers. Turns to mush. Transformation.
7This is buh · buh · Banana.
Apple and banana — both have white inside, but apple floats and banana sinks. Same colour inside, different in water.
🥭

Mango

mmm · mmm · Mango
1What colour? Yellow-orange. Between yellow and orange.
2Smell before cutting — sweet and strong.
3Cut with the toddler knife — what colour inside? Orange. Bright.
4What's in the middle? A big flat seed.
5Touch the flesh — slippery and soft.
6Taste it — sweet. Same as apple and banana.
7This is mmm · mmm · Mango. Hold the mmm sound for two seconds.
🍋

Lemon

lll · lll · Lemon
1What colour? Yellow — same as banana. Two yellow fruits, different shapes.
2Feel the outside — smooth or bumpy? Bumpy. Different from banana even though same colour.
3Smell before cutting — mild.
4Cut with the plastic knife when it arrives. What colour inside? Also yellow, but wet.
5Smell it now — completely different. Sharp and strong.
6Taste a tiny drop — sour face. New word: "Sour."
7Add bicarbonate — fizzes. Same as the vinegar sensory bins.
8This is lll · lll · Lemon. Hold the lll sound, tongue behind top teeth.
Save the lemon for end of month when the plastic knife arrives. The sour reaction will be the most memorable moment of the series.

Montessori practical life

Purposeful
hands

🥄

Ladle transfer across muffin tray

A ladle, a bowl of water or small objects, a muffin tray. Transfer one scoop per compartment. The size of the ladle relative to the compartment makes this genuinely challenging — both hands working, aim required. One-to-one correspondence built in naturally.

🫘

Spoon bean transfer

Chickpeas or dried beans, a spoon, two bowls. Scoop from one bowl, transfer to the other. The beans roll off the spoon — that productive difficulty is the whole point. Pincer grip already confirmed with chickpeas so the material is well matched.

Art

Making marks
this month

🍎

Fruit and vegetable stamp printing

Cut in half, dip in paint, press onto paper. Each produces a completely different print — apple reveals a star-shaped seed pattern, ladies finger reveals a perfect five-pointed star, broccoli floret makes a tree shape, potato makes a clean oval. Cut, look, name it, print it. The cross-section discovery is an inquiry moment before the art begins.

🪡

String art

Dip string in paint, drag across paper. The result is completely unpredictable — trails, pools, branches. Fold paper over the string and pull for a different effect. Drop string from a height. Each technique produces a different result from the same material. The unpredictability is what holds attention — he can't control the outcome fully, so every print is a discovery.

Playdough kitchen

Making &
shaping

🫙

Making the dough

He participates in the making — pouring, stirring, watching the transformation from powder to dough. Add food colouring to match fruit colours: red batch, yellow batch, orange batch.

🎭

Rolling and cutting

The KiwiCo pasta maker and cutters. Roll out, press, cut shapes. The rolling motion — both hands pressing outward — is new bilateral wrist work.

🔪

Knife practice on dough

Safe practice before cutting real fruit. Press down, feel the resistance, watch the dough separate. Build confidence on dough, transfer to banana and avocado with the toddler knife.

Sensory play

Colour-based
exploration

01

Colour mixing water

Red water and yellow water in separate jugs. Pour together — makes orange. Fruit month colour connection. "Red and yellow make orange — like a mango." The mixing moment is the payoff.

02

Colour sorting loose parts

Red, yellow, orange, green objects into matching colour bowls. Simple grouping by colour. Bridges directly into October colour month — the vocabulary being built now gets its dedicated month next.

03

Scent jars — Indian pantry

Crush a fresh curry leaf, coriander, mint, or ajwain into small jars. Hold to his nose one at a time — smell, name, react. "Curry leaf. Strong." "Mint. Fresh." No tasting required. The Indian pantry is a richer scent library than any synthetic essence — and the vocabulary is culturally his.

Keeping the mechanism alive

For the motorcycle
obsession

🔪

The knife is a mechanism

Press down, feel resistance, watch the fruit separate. Functional tool logic — exactly what captivates him. Lead with the mechanism, not the food vocabulary. The cutting is the interesting part.

⚙️

The pasta maker is a mechanism

Feed dough through, turn the handle, watch it emerge transformed. Cause and effect at a satisfying scale. The transformation from blob to strand is the same instinct that drives the oobleck investigations.

🚗

Keep one vehicle in the rotation

Transport month is over but his interest isn't. The flip car track, the cog wheel puzzle, the wooden vehicles — one mechanism toy alongside the fruit theme each week. The obsession doesn't need its own month to stay fed.

Early maths

How maths sounds
in September

Same / Different
"Both yellow — different fruits."
Big / Small
"Big mango, small grape."
Sink / Float
"Apple floats. Banana sinks."
Sweet / Sour
"Apple sweet. Lemon sour."
Smooth / Bumpy
"Apple smooth. Lemon bumpy."
More / Less
"More grapes on this side."

New this month — same and different

September introduces comparative classification. Both apple and banana are sweet — same. Apple floats and banana sinks — different. The inquiry format produces these comparisons naturally. The investigation IS the maths.
